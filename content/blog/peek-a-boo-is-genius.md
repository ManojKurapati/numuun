Around 6 to 9 months, something wild happens: your baby starts to understand
that things still exist when they can’t see them. Scientists call it
**object permanence**. You probably call it “the reason they cry when I go to
the bathroom.”

Peek-a-boo is the perfect workout for this brand-new skill.

## What your baby is learning

1. **Memory.** Your face disappears — and they have to hold the idea of you in
   their mind until it comes back.
2. **Prediction.** After a few rounds they start to anticipate the “boo!”.
   That giggle is the sound of a correct guess.
3. **Trust.** You leave, you come back. Every single time. That’s a big lesson
   wrapped in a small game.
4. **Turn-taking.** Watch for the moment they pull the blanket over *their own*
   face. Congratulations, you’re now the audience.

## Level it up

- **Hide a toy** under a cloth while they watch. Around 9 months, many babies
  will go looking for it.
- **Play with timing.** A long pause before “boo!” builds suspense. Babies
  love a plot twist.
- **Use a mirror.** Peek-a-boo with their own reflection is a whole new show.

> You don’t need fancy toys. You need a blanket, your face, and a willingness
> to look a little ridiculous. You’ve got this.
